# SprintLab AI Context

Generated by inspecting the current codebase (not hand-written product copy). This is the
AI-facing reference for SprintLab Intelligence — what the app actually stores, how the
deterministic planner reasons today, and what Gemini should treat as fixed vs. adjustable.
It intentionally excludes UI, styling, animation, and navigation details, which are irrelevant
to training reasoning.

## 1. Purpose

SprintLab is a local-first speed-training planner and workout log for track & field sprinters
and other athletes building linear speed (football, soccer, basketball, baseball/softball,
volleyball, general athletic performance). It builds a recurring weekly training schedule from
an athlete's profile, lets them execute and log workouts, and tracks history/progress over time.
All data today lives only on-device (AsyncStorage) — there is no backend, account system, or
sync. SprintLab Intelligence (this phase) is the first step toward adding a Gemini-backed coach
on top of this existing deterministic system.

## 2. Athlete profile (`AthleteProfile`, `types/domain.ts`)

Single record per device (`utils/athlete-profile.ts`, key `sprintlab.athlete-profile.v2`). Key fields:

- **Identity/demographics**: `name`, `ageRange`, `competitionCategory`, `competitionLevel`.
- **Sport**: `primarySport` (`AthleteSport`: track-and-field, football, soccer, basketball,
  baseball, softball, lacrosse, rugby, volleyball, general-athletic-performance, other),
  `sports[]` (can train for more than one), sport-specific sub-profiles
  (`trackProfile`, `footballProfile`, `soccerProfile`, `basketballProfile`,
  `baseballSoftballProfile`, `volleyballProfile`, `generalSpeedProfile`).
- **Events/PRs/goals**: `primaryEvent`/`secondaryEvents` (`SprintEvent`: 60m/100m/200m/300m/400m
  plus hurdles variants), `personalBests: PersonalBest[]` (`{ event, timeSeconds, date?,
  windMetersPerSecond?, indoor? }` — PRs are tied to a specific event, never a bare number),
  `speedGoals: SpeedGoal[]` (e.g. acceleration, maximum-velocity, speed-endurance),
  `raceDevelopmentAreas`, `targetPerformances`, `primaryGoal`.
- **Training level/tier**: `experienceLevel: AthleteExperienceLevel` = `beginner | developing |
  intermediate | advanced | elite`. The planner maps this to the workout library's own tier
  (`LibraryAthleteLevel` = `foundation | developing | trained | advanced`) via
  `profileLevel()` in `utils/plan-selector.ts`: beginner→foundation, developing→developing,
  intermediate→trained, advanced/elite→advanced. These two tier vocabularies are distinct types
  that get bridged at plan-build time — an AI reasoning about "level" should use whichever
  vocabulary matches the object it's looking at.
- **Schedule/availability**: `trainingDaysPerWeek`, `availableTrainingDays: TrainingDay[]`,
  `exactTrainingDaysPreference`, `preferredRestDay`, `usualSessionDurationMinutes`.
- **Access/equipment**: `trackAccess`/`grassAccess`/`hillAccess`/`indoorAccess`/
  `startingBlocksAccess`/`weightRoomAccess`/etc. (`AccessLevel` = none/occasional/regular),
  `homeEquipment: EquipmentType[]`.
- **Fixed commitments** (used to protect days from planning): `sportPracticeDays`,
  `gameOrCompetitionDays` (weekday name or an ISO date), `otherSportDays`, `busySchoolDays`,
  `currentTeamTrainingLoad`.
- **Physical constraints** (context, never a diagnosis): `currentPain`, `cautionAreas:
  PainArea[]`, `onboardingLimitations`, `trainingConcernDetails: TrainingConcernDetail[]`
  (`{ area, status: currently-limiting | recently-cleared | monitoring }`), `coachRestrictions`,
  `medicalRestrictions` (free text, recorded verbatim — never SprintLab's own medical judgment).
- **Season**: `seasonCalendar: SeasonCalendar` (see §9), `seasonPhaseOverride`.
- **Workflow**: `trainingPlanMode` (`build-my-plan | log-coach-plan | both`),
  `loggingOnlyMode` → resolved by `getTrainingWorkflow()` in `utils/athlete-profile.ts` into
  `sprintlab-plan | coach-plan | combined | log-only`. This determines whether SprintLab is
  allowed to generate/replace the athlete's schedule at all (see §12 hard constraints).
- `onboardingComplete: boolean` gates whether the app shows onboarding or the main tabs.

## 3. Workout data model

- **`PlannedWorkout`** (`types/index.ts`) — what's actually scheduled/executed: `id`, `title`,
  `purpose`, `durationMinutes`, `category?: WorkoutCategory`, `eventTags?: SprintEvent[]`,
  `sections: { title, exercises: PlannedExercise[] }[]`. Each `PlannedExercise` has an
  `ExerciseTracking` union: `completion` (simple check-off), `track` (`reps`,
  `distanceMeters?`, `targetIntensity?`, `restSeconds?`), or `strength` (`sets`, `targetReps`,
  `targetLoad?`, `restSeconds?`).
- **`LibraryWorkout`** (`types/workout-library.ts`) — the authored source content a plan is
  built *from* (`data/workout-library.ts`). Much richer: `primaryCategory` +
  `secondaryCategories` (`LibraryWorkoutCategory`, a larger taxonomy than the live
  `WorkoutCategory`), `eventPathways` (`short-sprint-100-200 | long-sprint-200-400 | shared`),
  `eventTags` (60m/100m/200m/400m only), `athleteLevels: LibraryAthleteLevel[]`,
  `seasonPhases: LibrarySeasonPhase[]`, `surface` requirements (required/preferred/prohibited),
  `metrics: WorkoutMetrics` (`totalSprintVolumeMeters`, `highIntensitySprintVolumeMeters`,
  `estimatedDurationMinutes`, `highCns: boolean`, `metabolicDemand`), `safetyNotes[]`,
  `modifications[]`, `progressionLevel`/`regressionWorkoutId`/`progressionWorkoutId`
  (progression chains between related workouts), and `approvalStatus:
  draft | approved | archived`. **Only `approved` library workouts are eligible for
  auto-generated plans** — this is enforced in the planner, not just a UI filter.
  `libraryWorkoutToPlannedWorkout()` converts a `LibraryWorkout` into the `PlannedWorkout`
  shape actually placed on the schedule.

## 4. Current week / plan data model

- **Live storage**: `ScheduledDay[]` (`utils/storage.ts`, key `sprintlab:week-schedule`) — one
  entry per weekday (`dayIndex: WeekdayIndex` 0=Sun..6=Sat), each `{ kind: 'workout' | 'rest',
  workout?, restTitle?, restNote? }`. This — not any richer "WeeklyPlan" domain type — is the
  actual recurring plan an athlete trains from. Read via `getWeekSchedule()`, written via
  `saveWeekSchedule()`. `hasSavedWeekSchedule()` distinguishes a real saved plan from the
  generic starter default (`data/workouts.ts`'s `defaultWeekSchedule`) shown before an athlete
  has saved anything.
- **History of the plan itself**: every save also appends a dated snapshot to
  `ScheduleHistoryEntry[]` (`{ effectiveFrom, schedule }`, key
  `sprintlab:week-schedule-history`). This lets the app resolve "what was actually scheduled on
  a past date" even after the recurring plan has since changed — important for any AI reasoning
  about historical adherence, since today's schedule is not a reliable proxy for a past date's
  schedule.
- **One-off future overrides**: `FutureWorkoutOverride[]` (key
  `sprintlab:future-workout-overrides`) — a specific date can carry a workout that overrides
  the recurring weekday pattern for that date only (e.g. duplicating a completed workout to a
  future date). Resolved ahead of the recurring schedule by `getScheduledDay(dayIndex, date)`.
- **The deterministic planner's own output shape** (`WeeklyPlanSuggestion`, `utils/plan-
  selector.ts`) is a *proposal*, not storage: `{ status: 'ready', schedule, suggestions:
  SuggestedPlanDay[], summary, warnings }` or a non-ready status (`coach-managed |
  unsupported-sport | no-match`) with an explanation. Each `SuggestedPlanDay` carries
  `whyThisFits[]`, `harderOptionsExcluded[]`, and `alternatives[]` — the planner already
  produces natural-language justification per day, which is a useful pattern for AI-generated
  explanations to match.
- A suggested week only becomes the athlete's real plan once explicitly saved
  (`saveWeekSchedule`) from the plan-preview review screen — nothing auto-commits.

## 5. Workout completion / history structure

- **`ActiveWorkoutSession`** (`types/index.ts`) — created by `startWorkoutSession()` when a
  workout begins: stable `id` (`session-${timestamp}`), `plannedWorkoutSnapshot` (a frozen copy
  of the plan at start time, so later library edits don't rewrite history),
  `scheduledDate?`/`scheduledDayIndex?` (present **only** when launched from an actual
  scheduled slot matching that date — Library/manual starts never set these), `readinessStatus`
  + `readinessSnapshot`, `startedAt`, `executionStartedAt` (execution-clock start, can differ
  from preview start), `elapsedSeconds`, `trainingContext`, and `actualResults:
  ActualExerciseResult[]` (per-exercise `status: pending|completed|skipped`, `trackReps[]` or
  `strengthSets[]` with per-rep/set actuals).
- **`CompletedWorkoutSession`** = `ActiveWorkoutSession` + `finishedAt`, `review:
  PostWorkoutReview` (`completed`, `rpe`, `energy`, `sleep`, `hamstring`, `soreness`,
  `bodyWeight?`, `notes`), `structuredLog?` (the richer `TrainingLog` domain shape). Stored via
  `addCompletedWorkoutSession()`, deduped by `id` so a double-save can't create two records.
- **Schedule linkage is the ground truth for "was this actually the planned session"**:
  a completed session only counts as fulfilling a scheduled day when its own `scheduledDate`
  exactly matches that date — never inferred from workout name, day-of-week, or timing
  coincidence. `utils/streaks.ts` implements this explicitly (`isLinkedCompletion`); any new AI
  logic reasoning about adherence must follow the same rule, not a looser same-day match.
- **`TrainingLogSummary[]`** (key `sprintlab:logs`) is a lighter list used by History's fast
  paths; **`TrainingLog[]`** (key `sprintlab:training-history`, domain shape) is the fuller
  per-session record (readiness, exercise-by-exercise results, surface/weather/wind, notes).
  Both are derived/kept in sync from the same completed session — they are not independent
  sources of truth.

## 6. Logs, RPE, readiness/recovery fields currently collected

- **Readiness check-in** (`ReadinessDecision`, one per date, key `sprintlab:readiness`):
  `sleep`, `sleepQuality`, `neuralReadiness`, `focus`, `foodStatus`, `hydrated`,
  `hasLocalizedIssue` + `sensation`/`location`/`painSeverity` if flagged,
  `hesitatesAtMaxEffort`, derived `readinessLevel: green | yellow | red` +
  `readinessReasons[]` + `readinessGuidance`, `warmupReassessment`,
  `maximalSprintRestricted: boolean`. Only the latest day's readiness is queryable directly;
  historical readiness still exists per completed session via `readinessSnapshot`.
- **Post-workout review** (`PostWorkoutReview`, one per completed session): `completed`
  (whether it went as planned), `rpe` (1–10), `energy`, `sleep`, `hamstring` soreness,
  `soreness` (general), `bodyWeight?`, free-text `notes`.
- **Per-rep/set actuals**: `TrackRepResult` (`timeSeconds?`, `feeling`, wind conditions,
  timing method) and `StrengthSetResult` (`load?`, `reps?`), each with their own
  `status: pending|completed|skipped`.
- **Prehab/recovery guidance** (`utils/prehab-engine.ts`) evaluates post-session recovery need
  from readiness + workout category into a `gate`: `clear | normal-soreness | modify-check |
  stop-refer`, with named recovery-exercise recommendations. This is advisory copy shown to the
  athlete after logging, not a code-enforced block.
- **Derived training signals** (`utils/streaks.ts`, `utils/progress.ts`, all fully recomputed
  from the above on every read, never separately persisted): Plan Streak (consecutive
  *scheduled* sessions completed, rest days neutral), Consistency Streak (consecutive weeks at
  ≥80% of that week's eligible scheduled sessions), weekly completion breakdown, sprint-time
  series, training-volume summaries, milestones.

## 7. Planner behavior (deterministic, `utils/plan-selector.ts`)

`buildDeterministicWeeklyPlan(profile, library)` is the current rule-based planner:

1. Resolves workflow (`getTrainingWorkflow`) — if the athlete is coach-managed or unsupported,
   returns a non-`ready` status with an explanation instead of guessing.
2. Maps the athlete to a candidate context: primary event/pathway, mapped athlete tier
   (§2), current season phase (§9), available surfaces/equipment.
3. Determines which weekdays are already blocked by fixed commitments
   (`blockedWeekdayReasons()` — preferred rest day, team practice, other sport, recurring or
   dated competitions/meets) and picks training days from the remaining pattern
   (`defaultDayPatterns`, keyed by `trainingDaysPerWeek`).
4. For track/field short- and long-sprint pathways, ranks and selects `approved` library
   workouts per weekday slot by category/event/phase/goal fit (`categoryScore`,
   `rankedForSlot`) and pairs a support (strength) workout where appropriate. Non-track sports
   route through a parallel "general speed" builder (`buildGeneralSpeedWeek`) using a tier
   system (`GeneralSpeedTier`) adjusted for recent training consistency.
5. `meetWindowAllowsWorkout()` (Season Engine, `utils/season-engine.ts`) restricts workout
   categories in the days immediately before a competition — this tightens as a meet gets
   closer and is stricter for A-priority meets/taper phase.
6. Produces a `WeeklyPlanSuggestion` with per-day justification (`whyThisFits`) — a *proposal*
   the athlete reviews and explicitly saves (§4), never auto-applied.
7. A separate `weekly-progression.ts` can propose reducing or advancing next week's load based
   on completed-session history (accepted/declined by the athlete on the review screen).

## 8. Workout-library structure

`LibraryWorkout[]` (`data/workout-library.ts`, `WorkoutLibraryState` with `schemaVersion` +
`seededVersion`) is the authored content catalog — see §3 for the shape. Organized around:
category taxonomy (`LibraryWorkoutCategory`, broader than the live `WorkoutCategory`), event
pathway/tag tags, athlete-level tags, season-phase tags, surface requirements, and progression
chains (`familyId`, `progressionLevel`, `regressionWorkoutId`/`progressionWorkoutId` linking
related workouts at different difficulty). Users can also author their own workouts via
`workout-builder.tsx`, which are not part of this authored/approved catalog and are never
auto-selected by the planner.

## 9. Season / phase information (`utils/season-engine.ts`)

`deriveSeasonPhase(profile)` computes a `SeasonPhaseResult` (`phase`, `explanation`,
`nextMeet`, `daysToNextMeet`, `daysToNextAMeet`) from `SeasonCalendar` (`competitionStatus`,
`seasonStartDate`/`seasonEndDate`, `firstMeetDate`, `championshipDate`,
`priorityMeets: PriorityMeet[]` each with `priority: A|B|C`). Phase order/thresholds:
`needs-calendar` (no calendar data at all) → `transition` (season ended ≤21 days ago) →
`taper` (A-priority meet ≤14 days away) → `competition` (inside the saved season window) →
`pre-competition` (next meet ≤56 days) → `specific-preparation` (next meet ≤112 days) →
`general-preparation` (default/fallback). A coach/editor `seasonPhaseOverride` (with a reason
and optional expiry) always wins over the computed phase. These exact day thresholds are
current implementation choices, not necessarily hard training-science boundaries — see §11.

## 10. Persistence/storage behavior relevant to AI

Everything is local-only `AsyncStorage`, no backend (see `utils/storage.ts` for the full key
list). Relevant patterns:
- Writes are last-write-wins per key; there is no multi-device sync or conflict resolution.
- Records that reference each other by id are kept in sync on write (e.g. editing a
  `TrainingLog` also patches the matching `CompletedWorkoutSession` and `TrainingLogSummary`;
  deleting one removes all three). Any AI-driven write path must follow the same pattern rather
  than editing one representation and leaving the others stale.
- `addCompletedWorkoutSession`/`addLog`/`addTrainingLog` all dedupe by stable id — the intended
  invariant is one record per real session, never duplicated by a retry.
- Dates are stored as local calendar-day strings (`toLocaleDateString('en-CA')` /
  `YYYY-MM-DD`), not UTC timestamps, and streak/weekly-adherence math is deliberately built
  around local calendar days for this reason.

## 11. Historical vs. future/editable data

- **Immutable-in-spirit / historical**: completed sessions (`CompletedWorkoutSession`,
  `TrainingLog`) and their `ScheduleHistoryEntry` context for the date they occurred. The app
  does allow an athlete to *edit* a saved log's actual results/notes (`updateTrainingLog`), but
  nothing should silently rewrite what a past date's schedule or completion record was as a
  side effect of changing today's plan.
- **Editable/future**: the recurring `ScheduledDay[]` plan, `FutureWorkoutOverride[]` for
  specific upcoming dates, the athlete profile, and any not-yet-reached day in the current week.
- **Live/derived, recompute-only**: streaks, weekly completion, volume summaries, milestones —
  never store a computed number as if it were a fact; recompute from source records.

## 12. Current app capabilities (for grounding, not exhaustive UI description)

Onboarding → deterministic plan suggestion → review/edit/save → Today (readiness check-in,
start scheduled or unplanned workout) → workout execution (per-exercise/rep/set tracking,
timers) → post-workout log (RPE/readiness fields, review) → History (searchable/filterable past
sessions, editable) → Progress (streaks, sprint-time trends, volume, milestones) → Settings
(profile edit, reminders, full local-data erase). A `POST /api/coach` Gemini proof-of-connection
endpoint exists (`app/api/coach+api.ts`) but is not wired to any UI or given athlete context yet.

## 13. Major invariants that must not be broken

- Only `approvalStatus: 'approved'` library workouts are eligible for auto-generated plan
  suggestions.
- A completed session only counts toward a scheduled day via exact `scheduledDate` match —
  never inferred from name/day-of-week/timing.
- A suggested plan is never applied to the athlete's real schedule without an explicit save.
- Coach-managed (`log-coach-plan`) and log-only athletes must never have SprintLab silently
  generate or overwrite their schedule.
- Readiness `red` + a workout requiring maximal sprinting is a hard start-block
  (`readinessBlocksWorkout` in `app/(tabs)/index.tsx`) — not advisory.
- `coachRestrictions`/`medicalRestrictions` are recorded verbatim and must never be
  reinterpreted, softened, or overridden by generated training content.
- No record should ever be duplicated by a retried save (id-based dedup is the existing
  mechanism — preserve it in any new write path).
- `GEMINI_API_KEY` stays server-side only; nothing athlete-identifying should be sent anywhere
  outside SprintLab's own `/api/*` routes without a deliberate, later decision to do so.

## Deterministic planner vs. AI — planning philosophy

The existing deterministic planner (`utils/plan-selector.ts` + `utils/season-engine.ts` +
the approved workout library) is a **reliable baseline and fallback**, not a ceiling on AI
reasoning. Its rules — day-pattern selection, category scoring, the exact phase-transition day
thresholds in §9, default weekly structure — were designed to produce a safe, predictable plan
*without* AI, and are often deliberately conservative or simplified so they work uniformly for
every athlete without judgment.

Gemini should treat the Season Engine, workout library, planner rules, and any research
documents placed in `knowledge/` as **important evidence and context** — a strong prior on what
good SprintLab training looks like — but should not assume every deterministic branch is an
absolute rule unless it clearly represents a genuine safety or data-integrity constraint (§13).

SprintLab AI should be allowed to reason beyond the deterministic planner when athlete-specific
context supports it, including: recent completed training, missed sessions, unusual
schedule/availability, goals/events, training level, season timing, recovery/readiness, recent
workload, meet timing, and direct athlete questions or constraints. A meaningful deviation from
the default planner should be explainable and grounded in available SprintLab knowledge and
athlete data — not invented. Do not fabricate training facts, physiology claims, or SprintLab
capabilities merely to appear more sophisticated or different from the deterministic planner.

### Hard constraints (must always hold)

- Data integrity: never silently rewrite a completed historical workout or its schedule
  context; never invent a record, field, or type not represented in the actual data model.
- Never create duplicate completion records for the same session.
- Never generate/overwrite a schedule for a coach-managed or log-only athlete.
- Respect the readiness hard-block on maximal sprinting and any explicit
  coach/medical restriction — these are safety boundaries, not preferences to weigh.
- Only `approved` library workouts may be presented as auto-generated training; never invent a
  workout that doesn't exist in the library or misrepresent a draft/archived workout as approved.

### Soft guidance (default, but reasonably overridable with grounded justification)

- Typical weekly day-pattern selection and default training-day count.
- Default workout tier/category chosen for a given slot.
- The exact day-count thresholds used for season-phase transitions (§9).
- Normal progression pacing and preferred weekly volume.
- Usual placement of a workout type within the week (e.g. which day gets the "hard" session).

If SprintLab Intelligence proposes something that deviates from soft guidance, it should say
why, referencing the athlete's actual data or the knowledge base — not just assert a different
answer.
